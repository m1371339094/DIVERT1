# Scam Patterns

This document is part of the Online Safety Companion knowledge base.
It is written at a pattern level to support risk assessment — not as a script.

---

## Romance Scams

A romance scam builds a fake emotional relationship to extract money.

**Common pattern:**
1. Contact on a dating app, game, or social media.
2. Rapid escalation of affection — "I feel such a strong connection with you."
3. Claims to be in the military, working overseas, or a professional traveller
   (reasons to never meet in person and to explain inconsistencies).
4. A manufactured crisis: medical emergency, legal trouble, business deal gone wrong.
5. Request for money: wire transfer, gift cards, cryptocurrency.
6. If the target sends money, further crises follow.

**Key signals:**
- Very attractive profile photos (often reverse-searchable stock images).
- Professes love or deep connection within days or weeks.
- Always has an excuse not to video-call or meet.
- Story details change over time.
- Never accepts the first "no" — escalates pressure.

## Gift Card Scams

The target is told they have won a prize, owe a debt, or must pay a fee.
Payment is requested via iTunes, Google Play, Amazon, Steam, or similar gift cards
because they are hard to trace and cannot be reversed.

**Key signals:**
- "You've been selected / you've won."
- "Pay a small processing fee to claim your prize."
- "Buy gift cards and send me the numbers on the back."
- Impersonates a government agency, employer, or tech company.

---

## Job and Investment Scams

Offers of easy money through modelling, brand deals, crypto investment,
or online work-from-home schemes.

**Key signals:**
- "No experience needed — I'll teach you everything."
- "Guaranteed returns on investment."
- Request to share bank details to "receive payments."
- Asks the target to recruit friends ("multi-level" structure).
- Requires an upfront payment or "starter kit" fee.

---

## Sextortion

The target is manipulated or tricked into sharing intimate images. The images
are then used as leverage to demand money or more images.

**Common pattern:**
1. Contact on a mainstream platform; quickly moves to a private app.
2. Romantic interest established rapidly.
3. Requests intimate images, often with reciprocal images (which are fake or stolen).
4. Once images are received: "Pay me or I'll send these to your family and school."

**Key signals:**
- Very fast escalation to intimate conversation.
- Pressure to use a platform with disappearing messages (Snapchat, Telegram).
- Requests for photos before any in-person meeting.
- Sudden change in tone after images are sent.

---

## Fake Urgency Pattern

Used across many scam types to prevent the target from thinking clearly
or consulting others.

**Phrases to watch for:**
- "This offer expires in 24 hours."
- "You must reply right now."
- "If you don't act immediately, you'll lose this forever."
- "There's no time — I need the gift card numbers now."

---

## Platform Migration Pressure

Scammers and groomers prefer platforms with less moderation, end-to-end
encryption, and disappearing messages.

**Common pattern:** Start on Discord, Roblox, a dating app, or Facebook.
Then: "Let's talk on Telegram / Snapchat / WhatsApp — it's more private."

The request itself is not always a red flag, but combined with other signals
it significantly raises risk.
