# Social Media Safety Guide for Young Adults
# Online Safety Companion v2 Knowledge Base

This document is for both the Safety Agent and the Support Agent.
It explains how online manipulation works on the platforms young adults
use most, in plain language.

---

## How Scammers and Groomers Find Young Adults Online

They look for:
- Public profiles with personal details (school, city, age, photos).
- Posts showing loneliness, stress, or a desire for connection.
- New accounts with few friends or followers.
- Young people in gaming servers, Discord communities, or fan groups.
- Profiles on dating apps where age is hard to verify.

---

## Red Flags by Platform

### Instagram and TikTok
- DMs from strangers who saw one post and claim to feel an instant connection.
- Accounts with very few posts but many followers (bought followers).
- "Collab" or "brand deal" offers from unknown accounts.
- Messages asking to move to another app quickly.
- Profile photos that look too perfect (may be AI-generated or stolen).

### Snapchat
- Requests for photos because "they disappear" — they do not always.
- People who only want to communicate on Snapchat and nowhere else.
- Friend requests from people who claim to know you through a mutual friend
  you cannot verify.
- Requests for intimate photos framed as "no big deal because it disappears."

### Discord
- Unsolicited DMs from strangers in a server.
- Offers of free Nitro, game keys, or rare items in exchange for clicking a link.
- "Staff" or "mod" accounts asking for your login or verification code.
- Requests to move to a private server or DM immediately.
- People who claim to be peers but whose story details keep changing.

### Roblox
- Offers of free Robux in exchange for your login details.
- Players who trade items generously early then ask for something in return.
- Requests to move to Discord, WhatsApp, or Snapchat.
- Players who ask where you live, go to school, or whether you are home alone.

### Dating Apps
- Profiles where the person claims to be young but writes like an adult.
- Someone who declares strong romantic feelings within the first few messages.
- Someone who always has an excuse not to video-call.
- Requests to send money to help with an "emergency."
- Pressure to move the conversation to Telegram or WhatsApp immediately.

## The Pressure Playbook — How They Escalate

Scammers and groomers follow a pattern:

1. **Find** — they search for someone who seems lonely, kind, or trusting.
2. **Build** — they give attention, compliments, gifts, and emotional support.
3. **Isolate** — they suggest keeping the relationship secret from family.
4. **Test** — they make a small request (a photo, a gift card, a secret).
5. **Push** — if the person complies, requests grow larger and more threatening.

Recognising the early steps makes it easier to stop the pattern before it escalates.

---

## Normal vs. Not Normal

### Normal in a new online friendship
- Talking about shared interests.
- Playing games together.
- Asking general questions about your life.
- Suggesting a video call to get to know each other better.

### Not normal — worth paying attention to
- Saying "I love you" within the first week.
- Asking you to keep the friendship secret.
- Asking you to move to a different, more private app.
- Asking for personal photos.
- Asking for money, gift cards, or your bank details.
- Saying your family "wouldn't understand" your friendship.
- Refusing to video-call or having the camera always broken.

---

## What Healthy Online Friendships Look Like

- Both people feel comfortable saying no.
- Neither person pressures the other to share more than they want to.
- The friendship is not kept secret from family or friends.
- Both people are comfortable with the pace of the relationship.
- Meeting in person (if it happens) is in a public place with other people present.
- Neither person has threatened the other.

## Quick Reference — Warning Signs

| Warning sign | What it usually means |
|---|---|
| "Don't tell your parents" | Secrecy request — a consistent grooming pattern |
| "Move to Telegram/Snapchat" | Platform migration — to avoid moderation |
| "I love you already" | Love-bombing — building artificial closeness fast |
| "Send me a photo" | May escalate to intimate image requests |
| "Send gift cards / Bitcoin" | Almost always a scam |
| "My camera is broken" | Avoids verification — may be hiding their identity |
| "You won a prize — click here" | Phishing or credential theft |
| Emergency story + money request | Romance or emergency scam |
| "Keep this between us" | Isolation and secrecy — a grooming pattern |
