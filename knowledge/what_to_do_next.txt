# What To Do Next

This document is part of the Online Safety Companion knowledge base.
It provides guidance for the Support Agent when advising users after a risk
assessment. It is written for use by the AI agent — not for direct display.

---

## Immediate Steps (any risk level)

1. **Stop replying for now.** You do not have to respond immediately.
   Taking time to think is always okay.
2. **Do not delete messages.** Even if the content feels embarrassing or
   upsetting, messages are evidence. Keep them.
3. **Take screenshots.** Capture the conversation, the person's profile,
   and any images or links they sent. Save them somewhere safe.

---

## Tell a Trusted Person

Telling someone can feel hard, especially if the conversation involved
intimate content. Here are some things that may help:

- You are **not in trouble**. What happened was not your fault.
- A trusted adult (parent, carer, teacher, school counsellor, support worker)
  can help you decide what to do next.
- You do not have to show them everything straight away — you can start
  by saying "I met someone online and I'm not sure if it's safe."
- You can also contact a helpline anonymously if you're not ready to talk
  to someone you know.

---

## Report on the Platform

Most platforms have a "Report" button on a profile or message.
Reporting does not automatically notify the person you are reporting.
Reporting helps protect other users too.

- Look for three dots (⋮), a flag icon, or "More options" near the message.
- Select "Report" and follow the steps.
- You can block after reporting to stop further contact.

## Official Reporting Resources

### United States
- **NCMEC CyberTipline** (online child exploitation, including sextortion):
  [report.cybertip.org](https://report.cybertip.org)
- **FBI IC3** (internet crime, including romance scams and financial fraud):
  [ic3.gov](https://www.ic3.gov)
- **FTC** (scams and fraud):
  [reportfraud.ftc.gov](https://reportfraud.ftc.gov)

### Canada
- **Canadian Centre for Child Protection** (child sexual abuse and exploitation):
  [cybertip.ca](https://www.cybertip.ca)
- **Canadian Anti-Fraud Centre** (financial fraud and scams):
  [antifraudcentre-centreantifraude.ca](https://www.antifraudcentre-centreantifraude.ca)

### United Kingdom
- **CEOP** (child sexual exploitation and grooming):
  [ceop.police.uk](https://www.ceop.police.uk)
- **Action Fraud** (financial fraud):
  [actionfraud.police.uk](https://www.actionfraud.police.uk)

### Australia
- **eSafety Commissioner**:
  [esafety.gov.au](https://www.esafety.gov.au)
- **Scamwatch**:
  [scamwatch.gov.au](https://www.scamwatch.gov.au)

## If You Feel Unsafe Right Now

- **US / Canada**: Call or text **988** (Suicide and Crisis Lifeline — also
  helps people in any mental health or safety crisis).
- **UK**: Call **116 123** (Samaritans, 24/7, free).
- **Emergency**: Call **911** (US/Canada), **999** (UK), or **000** (Australia).

---

## If Money Was Sent

- Contact your bank immediately. Ask them to try to reverse the transaction.
- If you used a gift card: contact the gift card company and report it as fraud.
- If you used cryptocurrency: contact the exchange and report the transaction.
- Document everything: screenshots, transaction IDs, the person's profile.

---

## If Intimate Images Were Shared

- Do not pay any demands. Paying rarely stops the threats.
- Report to the platform and to NCMEC (US) or the Canadian Centre for Child
  Protection (Canada) using the links above.
- StopNCII ([stopncii.org](https://stopncii.org)) can help prevent images from
  spreading across participating platforms.
- Talk to a trusted adult or a helpline. You do not have to face this alone.
